How InhaleToEat Works

Finding Your ACR & Protein Factor
Forget injected ratios — Afrezza's hepatic bias and fast clearance mean your numbers will look 'weaker' (higher ACR), and that's normal for smooth control.

Step 1: Tune ACR with carb-focused meals
• Test a known carb load (e.g., 30g rice or bread, low protein/fat).
• Dose upfront with the app's 'NOW' recommendation.
• Monitor BG rise/drop.
• Adjust ACR: higher if you go low, lower if high.
• Typical range: 30–45g carbs per 4u. Many optimized users land 35–40.
• For these tests, use the XS protein preset — it's fixed at 0g, for meals that are essentially "naked carbs" (plain rice, bread, fruit, a starchy side with nothing to slow it down). That keeps the test honest: Small still carries enough protein to shift the math, which isn't what you want when you're isolating ACR alone.

Step 2: Tune Protein Factor with zero-carb meals
• Eat high-protein, zero-carb (e.g., 300g steak or chicken — vary fat/speed sliders).
• Use the app for dose & split. Monitor 4–5 hours.
• Late high → increase PGF (0.65–0.75 typical for low-carb).
• Low → decrease PGF.

Pro tips
• Start conservative: Use a higher ACR (40–50) especially if new to Afrezza, sensitive (honeymoon/LADA), or insulin-naïve — this mimics the old Ultra Conservative mode safely.
• Log 5–10 meals before finalizing numbers.
• Tweak ACR by 5, PGF by 0.05.
• Ratios can vary by time of day, activity, or illness — trust what keeps you in range.
Digestion Mode: Average vs Fast
This is your personal baseline metabolic speed — separate from the Protein Speed and Fat Load sliders, which describe the meal. Digestion Mode describes you, and it's saved per profile, just like ACR.

Average Digester — standard timing. Start here. It's the right default for most people, including anyone new to Afrezza.

Fast Digester ⚡ — quicker, less delay. Shifts more of the dose upfront and shortens the gap before any follow-up dose. This fits people whose CGM shows an early spike followed by a crash if they wait for the app's normal delayed-dose timing — i.e., their own gut empties faster than the average assumption.

• Don't guess at this one — switch to Fast only after you've seen a consistent early-spike-then-crash pattern on Average, ideally over a few meals, not just one.
• You can flip it per meal if your response genuinely varies day to day, but for most people it's a "set once" choice.
🍕 Pizza Mode
A dedicated toggle for complex, high-fat, high-carb meals — pizza, fried food with carbs, buffets, holiday plates. With meals like that, the fat load is the real problem, more than the protein: it drags out digestion well past what a single follow-up dose can cover.

What it changes: carbs get split pre/later the same way protein already is, and a second, later follow-up checkpoint appears (staggered further out than the normal one) to catch the extended glycemic wave.

What it doesn't change: normal-mode dosing math is completely untouched when this toggle is off — Pizza Mode is opt-in, per meal.

• It's a one-meal toggle, not a saved setting — turn it on right before dosing that specific meal, and it resets afterward.
• If you're not sure a meal qualifies, ask: "is this high in both fat and carbs?" If it's just one or the other, you probably don't need it.
Reading Your Dose: Cartridge Chips
Every dose is shown as real Afrezza cartridge colors, not just a number — 4u is blue, 8u is green, 12u is yellow, matching the actual cartridges in your hand. The unit number is always printed on the chip too, since color alone isn't reliable to sort out under poor lighting.

Doses are always built from the fewest cartridges possible — largest sizes first — so a 16u dose shows as one yellow (12) + one blue (4), never four blues. If a single dose ever needs more than six cartridges of the same size, the chips collapse into one chip marked "×N" instead of drawing that many individually, just to keep the display readable.
Meal Log, Outcomes & the Printed Report
Nothing is saved automatically — tap 💾 Save this meal only for the meals you actually want a record of. Each profile keeps its 15 most recent saved meals; once you hit 15, the oldest one drops off to make room for the next.

Once you know how a saved meal turned out, open 📋 Meal Log and tag it Low, On target, or High. That's for you and your provider to read patterns from — the app never scores, calculates, or suggests a change based on these tags. Tapped the wrong tag, or logged a duplicate by mistake? Tap 🗑 Delete on that entry to remove it.

Printable report: one tap turns your current log into a plain black-and-white table — ready to hand to, or save as a PDF for, a healthcare provider. It includes the ACR, Protein Factor, Basal dose, and Today's Adjustment that were actually in effect for each meal, plus your set target range in the header (flagged if that range changed partway through the meals shown, so nothing is read against the wrong target by mistake).
Current BG & Trend Flag
A dose recommendation that's blind to where you actually are right now is missing something real — 4u might be exactly right at 110 and rising, and a genuinely bad idea at 75 flat or falling. This is an optional pair of fields (Current BG, and a Rising / Flat / Falling trend) that let the app notice that gap and put up a flag — nothing more.

What it does: compares what you enter against your Target Range setting, and shows a red "check before dosing" banner if BG is at or below your target-range low, or in the bottom third of your range with a Flat or Falling trend.

What it deliberately doesn't do: calculate a correction, suggest a smaller or larger dose, or touch the dosing math in any way — that's a different, much higher-stakes kind of tool, and outside what this app is for. A sharp, fast trend is a candidate for an actual correction dose first, which is squarely a conversation with your CGM and your provider, not this app.

• Leave both fields blank if you don't want this — nothing changes, no flag, no friction.
• It resets when you switch profiles, since a BG reading only means something for the person it came from.
• The point isn't to lean on this forever — it's to catch the edge case while you're still building your own judgment for when a dose needs a second look.
